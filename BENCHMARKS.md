# Product benchmark notes

Checked 4 October 2026 against official product pages. These are public-feature observations and design hypotheses, not hands-on efficacy comparisons or assertions that competing products fail to teach.

- [HelloChinese](https://www.hellochinese.cc/) advertises a broad beginner curriculum, speech recognition, native-speaker videos and spaced repetition. Take the integration of useful beginner tasks seriously. Hypothesis to test here: adults benefit from calmer feedback and clearly separated recognition versus spontaneous use. No comparative superiority claim.
- [Du Chinese](https://duchinese.net/) emphasizes graded stories, word lookup and native audio. Borrow the continuity and low-friction help principle, not the content. Protect listening-only attempts from text exposure and deliberately bridge to response generation.
- [Pleco](https://www.pleco.com/) establishes the importance of reliable lexical reference. Our 78-item seed lexicon is explicitly a course dictionary, not a Pleco replacement. Keep natural usage and sources visible.
- [Anki](https://docs.ankiweb.net/) offers durable retrieval practice and control. Use a maintained FSRS implementation and distinguish modalities rather than equating card familiarity with conversation.

## Wider public-feature scan

These observations also use official sources, checked on the same date. Proposed adaptations are our hypotheses, not judgments of competitor efficacy.

- [Duolingo](https://en.duolingo.com/) describes integrated reading, writing, listening and speaking activities; its [Chinese explainer](https://blog.duolingo.com/chinese-languages/) clarifies the course language/script. Adapt a clear next action and quick task entry. Test whether calm encouragement motivates this adult audience without a points economy.
- [SuperChinese](https://www.superchinese.com/landing) advertises AI support and speech recognition. A responsive speech loop is relevant; our implementation must distinguish transcript recognition from independently validated pronunciation evidence.
- [ChineseSkill](https://www.chineseskill.com/) offers a structured app experience across Chinese varieties. Make Mandarin scope explicit and keep beginner contexts easy to navigate. Full comparative curriculum and language-variant coverage were not independently audited.
- [The Chairman’s Bao](https://www.thechairmansbao.com/) combines graded news and human-recorded article audio. Pair interesting, level-appropriate input with listening and response tasks; license content before integrating it.
- [ChinesePod](https://www.chinesepod.com/) offers dialogue-based audio/video lessons with characters, pinyin and translations. Use meaningful social context and replayable short segments, while distinguishing a teaching explanation from actual listening evidence.
- [Pimsleur Mandarin](https://www.pimsleur.com/learn-chinese-mandarin/) describes audio instruction and interactive/offline practice. Preserve an ears-and-voice path through the day; test retrieval latency and spontaneous transfer separately from lesson completion.
- [Glossika Mandarin](https://ai.glossika.com/language/learn-chinese-beijing) emphasizes sentence practice and native-speaker audio. Repeated useful chunks and voice variation are relevant. Our seed pack does not match its advertised content breadth or native audio and makes no such claim.
- [LingQ Chinese](https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-chinese-online/) links audio/transcript content, personal imports and contextual vocabulary review. Support learner interests and local media now, then add licensed persistent phrase/segment workflows rather than claiming that a file picker is an equivalent library.
- [Speak](https://www.speak.com/) emphasizes learning phrases, repeated production and AI conversation. Use intention-driven interaction and explicit feedback boundaries. Our bounded scripted matcher is not presented as equivalent to its conversational service.

## Research boundary and next benchmark

This is a 13-product public-feature scan. No paid subscriptions, multiweek use, internal retention metrics, blinded pronunciation comparisons or comparative efficacy study were conducted. Product positioning and vendor claims are not proof of outcomes. The next hands-on study should compare a beginner’s first 30 minutes, time to first meaningful response, support use, unfamiliar-voice comprehension, review after seven days, accessibility and recovery from incorrect speech. Record observed behavior and quote direct evidence; do not invent weaknesses from marketing copy.

The initial product hypothesis is integration: listening, retrieval, repair, purposeful conversation and media support in one learner record. Validate it with real English- and Vietnamese-speaking learners before superiority or fluency claims.
